Record 01

Policy, but make it practical

Public policy and institutional design

I am interested in the point where a policy leaves the briefing paper and reaches the person expected to use it. Through ComLink+ and community work, I have become more attentive to the barriers created by language, administrative complexity, housing, caregiving and limited resources.

I want to study whether programmes achieve their intended outcomes, how implementation gaps are identified and how feedback from affected communities reaches decision-makers.

Questions I want to investigate
  • How accessible are family and social-support schemes in practice?
  • How should programme outcomes be measured beyond participation numbers?
  • Where do legal entitlement, policy design and frontline delivery diverge?

Family law interests me because a legal problem can involve safety, housing, caregiving, maintenance and relationships that continue after the proceedings end. My interest developed through family-law studies, exposure to matrimonial matters in practice, community work with families and helping a relative understand Syariah maintenance issues.

I want to understand how family justice can remain procedurally fair while being usable by self-represented persons and responsive to financial abuse, coercive control and caregiving realities.

Questions I care about
  • How accessible are family justice processes without legal representation?
  • How do maintenance, housing and caregiving policies interact?
  • What makes a legal remedy practically usable?

Commercial law interests me because it connects legal reasoning with the machinery of an organisation. In practice, I have seen how contracts, invoices, payment obligations, corporate records, regulatory requirements and dispute risk depend on accurate information moving between people.

My work has included supporting corporate and commercial matters, preparing documents and correspondence, handling billing and payment tracking, and coordinating with lawyers, banks, accountants, clients and government bodies.

What I want to develop
  • Contract review and commercial drafting
  • Corporate governance and regulatory compliance
  • Preventive legal operations before disputes arise

Privacy law interests me because compliance is rarely a simple checklist. In my GDPR and PDPA project, I compare how identical policy wording can produce different conclusions under two regulatory systems.

The work examines consent and other legal bases, controller and processor roles, data intermediaries, purpose limitation, retention and cross-border transfers. My contribution includes comparative legal research, compliance logic, ambiguity mapping and product direction.

Next questions
  • When should an automated tool flag ambiguity instead of declaring non-compliance?
  • How can privacy advice remain legally accurate and understandable?
  • How do regulatory differences affect cross-border services?

Beyond law and policy, I love physical media. I began collecting in 2023 and have since built a growing collection of vinyl records, cassettes and CDs. I enjoy the deliberate experience of selecting a record, handling the sleeve and listening to an album in sequence. Michael Jackson, the Eagles, Bryan Adams and Bring Me The Horizon can all appear in the same listening session.

I am also an avid reader. Even during busier periods, I try to complete at least one book and make time to explore subjects beyond my formal studies. Coffee is usually involved, whether I am reading, researching or spending far too long reorganising an idea.

Although naturally introverted, I value meeting people and engaging in meaningful conversations. Learning how others understand the world exposes me to perspectives that I would not encounter independently and often gives me new questions to consider.

My creative interests extend into music and technology. I play the electric guitar and hope to learn the violin. I also enjoy experimenting with programming languages, debugging code and building digital projects. Resolving a technical problem gives me the same satisfaction as untangling a difficult legal or factual issue.

I remain open to further volunteering opportunities, particularly those that allow me to contribute to the community while meeting people with similar interests in service, law and social issues.

Policy and research

The work I want to move towards

I am not looking for a policy internship simply because “policy” sounds interesting. I want research roles where I can examine how legal rules and public programmes behave after implementation, especially for people navigating financial, informational or institutional barriers.

Priority 01

Family and social policy

Family support, rental housing, caregiving, social mobility and the accessibility of schemes intended for vulnerable households.

Priority 02

Access to justice

Whether people can understand, afford and practically use legal processes, particularly in family and employment-related matters.

Priority 03

Labour and migrant-worker policy

Wage protection, employment rights, legal assistance and the position of lower-wage migrant workers within Singapore's labour system.

Priority 04

Digital regulation and privacy

PDPA, GDPR, cross-border data governance and the consequences of regulatory differences for organisations and individuals.
