Record 01

Policy, but make it practical

Public policy and institutional design

I am interested in public policy because rules do not land equally on everyone. A scheme can look complete on paper and still be difficult to understand, access or use.

My community work made that gap much harder to ignore. I want to learn how policy is researched, designed, communicated and reviewed, especially in social policy, family support and access to justice.

Currently looking forPublic policy internshipsResearch assistant opportunitiesAdvocacy and think tank projects

Family law interests me because legal problems rarely arrive alone. They come with money, housing, caregiving, fear and relationships that still have to continue after the file closes.

I want to understand how the law can protect vulnerable family members while remaining practical for the people expected to navigate it.

I enjoy commercial law because it reveals the machinery behind everyday business. Contracts, risk, payment, procedure and documentation all have to move together.

Working in small legal teams taught me how much good legal work depends on accurate operations, clear records and people who notice what is missing.

I like testing where law and technology can genuinely help each other. My GDPR and PDPA project asks how the same privacy wording can lead to different legal conclusions across jurisdictions.

I also build digital things because design can make dense information feel less hostile. This website is one of those experiments.

Outside law, I collect old songs, learn electric guitar, build unnecessarily elaborate digital projects and analyse almost everything. Michael Jackson, the Eagles, Bryan Adams and Bring Me The Horizon can all appear in the same queue.

I am introverted, curious and happiest in conversations where both people leave with a new question.
