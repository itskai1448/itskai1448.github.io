Behind the records

When I say I study rules and the people living with them, I think about how differently I’ve encountered the same subject. I first learned about employment law as an HR student at ITE. Later, as a volunteer with HOME, I sat beside a migrant worker as a Court Friend at the Employment Claims Tribunals. By then, the question was specific to one person: what had happened, which records helped explain it, and how could we put the events in order?

I’m now studying Legal Executive Studies part-time at Temasek Polytechnic. Working alongside my studies has given me the chance to follow some of those questions further and discover a few I’d like to pursue next.

Why in-house legal and compliance?

If you picked Invincible from the shelf, you’ll have found the privacy compliance analyser I’ve been building with Edoardo. My interest in data protection began when I encountered the PDPA during my HR studies. At Dun & Bradstreet Singapore, I’ve since researched how Singapore and Malaysia approach personal data and AI. What I enjoy about working in-house is having a reason to go one question further: given what the research says, what does the business need to do? I like the practical side of that work too. I’ve completed a contract master register that others can use, and I’ve reviewed compliance records where the most useful thing I could do was explain exactly what the evidence showed and what still needed checking.

Why finance and corporate strategy?

M&A interests me because I want to understand why a company would pursue a deal in the first place. What does the buyer see in the business? Which assumptions have the greatest effect on its valuation? When would it make more sense to invest in the company’s own operations? I can bring experience in research, document review and reconciling records. What I want to learn next is how an investment or corporate planning team weighs the evidence and arrives at a decision.

Why social policy?

At HOME, I may meet a worker because they need support with an employment matter. On weekends at OKA, I meet migrant workers who have come to take part in a community activity. As a ComLink+ befriender, a conversation might be about something as ordinary as choosing school subjects. I value all three settings because people tell you different things when you give them room to decide what matters that day. That’s also what draws me to policy research. When several people describe a similar difficulty, I want to understand what they have in common, whose experience we might be missing, and whether a proposed change would help in practice.

What can you bring me in to do?

Give me a question that needs investigating. I can work through the documents, follow up with the people involved, and write an account that makes clear what we know, what remains uncertain, and what someone can do next.

And if you came here to see my work and leave as a Michael Jackson fan, I’ll count that as a win too. My case for why HIStory deserves more love is available on request. 💿
